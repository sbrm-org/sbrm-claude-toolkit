# Protocol: HR / Operational Policy

## Scoping Questions

Ask these before drafting (or state reasonable defaults if context is clear):

1. **Which employees?** All staff, specific classifications (exempt/non-exempt), volunteers, contractors?
2. **Employer size?** Under 25, 25-49, 50-99, 100+? (Triggers different regulatory thresholds)
3. **Existing policies?** Replacing, updating, or creating net-new? Any current language to preserve?
4. **Union status?** CBA provisions that constrain or override policy?
5. **Approval path?** Board approval required, or executive authority sufficient?

**Default assumptions (California nonprofit, non-union, board approval for new policies — adjust to your organization).**

## Required Sections (in order)

1. **Purpose** -- Why this policy exists (1-2 sentences)
2. **Scope** -- Who it applies to and when it took effect
3. **Definitions** -- Key terms used in the policy
4. **Policy Statement** -- The actual policy rules
5. **Procedures** -- Step-by-step implementation (who does what, when)
6. **Responsibilities** -- Role-specific duties (employee, supervisor, HR, executive)
7. **Enforcement** -- Consequences of non-compliance
8. **Exceptions** -- How to request exceptions and who approves
9. **Review Schedule** -- When and who reviews this policy (annual minimum)
10. **Regulatory References** -- Statutes, regulations, agency guidance cited
11. **Assumptions & Verification Needed** -- What was assumed without source; what needs legal review
12. **Implementation Checklist** -- Concrete steps to operationalize (training, forms, system updates)

**Version block at top:**
```
Policy: [Name]
Version: [1.0]
Effective: [Date]
Last Reviewed: [Date]
Approved By: [Name/Title or "Board of Directors"]
Next Review: [Date]
```

## Acceptance Tests (Definition of Done)

- [ ] All 12 required sections present and non-empty
- [ ] Jurisdiction explicitly stated (e.g., "California" or "Federal")
- [ ] "Consult counsel" flag present for any item creating potential employer liability
- [ ] Version/date block at top of document
- [ ] Implementation checklist with concrete action items (not generic)
- [ ] Regulatory references include statute numbers, not just agency names
- [ ] No confident unsourced regulatory claims (source cited or "needs verification" label)
- [ ] Definitions section covers every term used in a specialized or legal sense
- [ ] Enforcement section specifies progressive discipline or refers to existing discipline policy

## Common Pitfalls

- Forgetting California-specific requirements that exceed federal minimums
- Missing local ordinances (SF, LA, SD, Santa Barbara have additional requirements)
- Not addressing the interaction between policies (e.g., sick leave + FMLA + ADA)
- Writing enforcement language that's too rigid (removes management discretion) or too vague (unenforceable)
- Omitting implementation steps -- policy gets approved but never operationalized
